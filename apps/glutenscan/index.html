01

What it does

A clear answer in the aisle, not a guess at home.

Scan any barcode

Point your camera at the barcode, or type the number when the label is crumpled.

A clear verdict

Certified gluten-free, labeled gluten-free, likely safe, may contain, or contains gluten.

Ingredient by ingredient

See exactly which ingredients raised a flag and which ones checked out.

Catches hidden gluten

Spots wheat, barley, rye and spelt, plus malt, brewer’s yeast and oats that may be cross-contaminated.

Honest about unknowns

If there is no ingredient data, GlutenScan says so. It never calls a product safe without it.

Scan history

Every scan is saved on your phone. Search past products and see your monthly totals.

03

Questions

More help is on the Support page.

Can GlutenScan replace medical advice?

No. It reads labels. Check with your doctor about your diet.

Where does the product information come from?

From Open Food Facts, a free, open database of food products from around the world.

What if a product has no ingredient list?

You get an Unknown result. GlutenScan never calls a product safe when it cannot see the ingredients.

What is the difference between certified and labeled gluten-free?

Certified means a third party such as GFCO has tested it. Labeled means the manufacturer says it is gluten-free.

Does it understand oats and buckwheat?

Yes. Oats get a cross-contamination warning unless they are certified, and buckwheat is not mistaken for wheat.

Where are my scans stored?

On your phone. Your scan history stays on your device.
